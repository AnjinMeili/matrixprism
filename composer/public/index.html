Spectrum Lab

Fractional-octave bands are anchored at 1 kHz; their count follows the selected frequency range. FFT size sets real resolution, not the number of display bars. Larger FFTs resolve bass better but react more slowly.

Positive slope boosts highs relative to 1 kHz. Analysis gain is separate from Reactivity. Ballistics and peak hold follow real audio time, not animation Speed. Curves are visual interpolation, not extra frequency resolution.

Levels are relative digital FFT magnitudes, not calibrated sound pressure. Web Audio uses a Blackman window. Bands narrower than a bin share unresolved information. Maximum frequency is capped at the input Nyquist limit.

Playlists

An entry is a saved look: the layer A pattern, the mixer state (layer B's pattern, blend and amount), both layers' shape and region, each layer's Pattern settings, and the look settings: hue, speed, palette and palette cycling, key colours, flock count, dot size, Sparkles and Spectrum Lab. Set the screen up the way you want it, then use Add current look, or Hotsave below. While a playlist plays it replaces Auto cycle; changing pattern crossfades as usual, and the rest switches with it.

Brightness, gamma and frame rate are not stored, as they suit the panel rather than the look. Entries saved before 2026-10-16 keep only the patterns, mixer and shapes, and leave the rest as it is. Playlists are kept in this browser with the other settings.

Hotsave

Press the key anywhere on the page, except while typing in a field, to add the look on screen to a playlist as a new entry named for the time, with everything an entry keeps. It goes to a playlist called Hotsave, made on first use, unless another is chosen. Whatever is playing carries on. The key is one letter or digit; T taps the tempo.

Randomizer

Each ticked item changes on its own timer, waiting a new random time between the shortest and longest wait after every change, counted in seconds, or in beats or bars of the tempo, when a wait is a whole number of them and ends on a beat or a bar line, so the layers and their attributes drift apart rather than changing together. Layer B items only run while the mixer is on. A lone layer keeps the whole panel; regions are only randomized while the mixer is on. The Mixer's Right works the same way: input C always, input D and Right's blend while Right is mixing (its fader moved or a blend chosen).

The randomizer pauses while a playlist is playing, and takes the place of Auto cycle when it is changing layer A's pattern.

Tap pad

The live input folded by the beat clock's bar: each column is one bar, newest at the right, with marks at each beat. Straight lines mean the clock keeps time with the music; sloping lines, its tempo is off; level lines between the marks, its phase is off. It keeps building whatever pattern is showing.

To find the tempo by eye, nudge: the last minutes of sound are folded again at the tempo shown, at one steady tempo across the picture, until the lines stand level. The bar line moves the rows, so the first beat sits on the top mark. Send to the tracker gives the tempo and bar line to the beat tracker, which goes on following the music from there and keeps them until the music says otherwise; Make it the manual tempo holds the clock there whatever the music does (Detect hands it back); Follow the clock goes back to its own graph.

The Camelot wheel: minor keys (A) inside, major (B) outside, 12 at the top. Every key is shaded by how well it fits the last several seconds of pitched music, brighter the better; the key in use is lit and ringed in white, its mixing neighbours (one either side, and the other ring at the same number) outlined, and a dot marks the detector's own best when it hears another key.

Click a key to say what you hear. The detector takes it and keeps it until the music clearly says another key for eight seconds; the ring is dashed while the key is yours. With "Keep the key I choose" ticked it stays whatever the music says. Detect forgets it and lets the detector decide again.

A rolling major/minor estimate: allow several seconds of pitched music. Silence, percussion and ambiguous harmony may not give a key. Key colour adds to the manual hue shift.

Audio

The tempo drives each input's spin and pulse (Mixer, each input's More…), and can hold pattern changes from Auto cycle, the playlist and the randomizer until the next beat or bar.

Silence means nothing heard for five seconds, or the input set to None.

The musical key, its wheel and its settings are on the Monitor view.

For music playing on this computer, route it through a loopback device such as BlackHole and choose that input.

Persistent overlays

Clocks, dates and text stay above every pattern and crossfade, in the preview and LED output. Elements later in the list draw on top. Position, offsets and text size use native panel pixels. Oversized elements are clipped at the panel edges.

Clocks use this computer's wall time even at Speed zero. Overlay colours ignore pattern hue; global brightness and gamma still apply. Set background opacity to zero for transparent text. Up to 16 elements; settings persist across reloads.
